ArcQuote privacy policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

ArcQuote is made by 77 Cube ("we"). This policy says exactly what the app does with what you put into it. It describes the app as it works today; if that changes, this page changes with it and the date above moves.

The short version: your jobs, your customers and your prices stay on your phone. We have no server, no accounts, and no way to read your work.

What stays on your phone

Everything you enter lives in a database inside the app's own private storage, where other apps cannot reach it:

  • jobs, quotes and estimates, line items, prices, costs, markups and tax settings
  • customer names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and any notes you write
  • job photos, the PDFs of quotes you have issued, and your logo
  • timer entries, close-outs and what the app learns from your finished jobs
  • whether ArcQuote Pro is active on this phone

We never receive any of it. There is no ArcQuote account and no ArcQuote server.

What leaves your phone, and only then

Quotes you send. When you share a quote, the PDF goes wherever you send it: email, messaging, Drive, a printer. That is your doing, and the file goes straight from your phone.

Backups. Android's own backup copies the ArcQuote database to your Google Drive, under your Google account, so a new phone can pick up where the old one left off. Photos and issued PDFs are left out of it. That copy is governed by Google's terms, not ours, and we cannot see it. You can switch Android backups off in your phone's settings. Backup files you export yourself go wherever you choose to put them.

Crash reports (optional, on by default). When ArcQuote hits an error, a report goes to Sentry so we can fix it. Before it is sent, the app strips out anything that could describe your work: customer names, job titles, notes and amounts are removed, and database errors are cut off before the values they were handling. What remains is the kind of error, where in the code it happened, the app version and your Android version and device model. Switch it off under Settings → Backup and privacy.

Usage counts (optional, on by default). So we know which parts of the app are actually used, ArcQuote sends Aptabase the name of an action — for example that a quote was sent — together with the week you installed the app. No names, no amounts, no identifier that follows you. Aptabase also derives your app version, operating system and country from the connection. Switch it off in the same place.

Purchases. ArcQuote Pro is sold through Google Play. Google handles the payment; we never see your card, your billing address or your Google account. The app only records on this phone whether a subscription is active.

We do not show ads, we do not use advertising identifiers, we do not track you across apps or websites, and we do not sell or share anything with anyone else.

Permissions the app asks for

ArcQuote holds as few as it can. These are all of them:

  • Notifications, for the running job timer and for reminders you set. ArcQuote never sends marketing notifications.
  • Internet and network state, used only for the optional crash reports and usage counts above, and by Google Play for purchases.
  • Run at start-up, so the reminders you have set still arrive after the phone restarts.
  • Vibrate, for those reminders.
  • Billing, for the Google Play subscription.

ArcQuote does not hold the camera or photo permissions. Adding a photo hands you to your phone's own camera app or photo picker, which gives the app the one picture you chose; its location and camera details are stripped out before it is stored.

How long anything is kept

Your data stays on your phone until you delete it or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes everything ArcQuote stored, so keep a backup file first if you want to keep your work.

Crash reports and usage counts are kept by Sentry and Aptabase under their own retention periods, typically a few months, and are not linked to you.

Children

ArcQuote is a tool for working welders and is not directed at children.

Your rights

Because we hold almost nothing about you, most requests are answered on your own phone: delete a job, delete the app, or switch off crash reports and usage counts. If you want a crash report or usage record removed, or you have a question, write to us and say roughly when it happened, and we will have it deleted.
